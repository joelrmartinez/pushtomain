# How I run a cold-calling pilot, week by week

> A cold-calling pilot is a short test with one job: find out if calling a specific list can book meetings at a cost that works for you. I run them over a few weeks. At the end you have a number, the reasons behind it, and a decision to make. This is how the weeks go, so you know what you'd be buying before we talk.

## Before week one: write down the question

Most pilots go wrong before anyone dials, because nobody wrote down what was being tested. "See if calling works" isn't a question. "Can we book this many meetings with this title at this kind of company in this many weeks" is.

So we start from your revenue goal and work backward. It's the same math as my [Math of Sales calculator](https://pushtomain.xyz/work/math-of-sales/): deal size, close rate, show rate, how often a conversation turns into a meeting, and how often someone picks up. That tells us how many dials it takes to matter. If the answer is more dials than your team could ever make, better to learn it on day one than in month three.

What I need from you:

- The intake brief, or a call where we go through it
- A list to work, or the go-ahead for me to build one
- An exclusion list of customers and open deals
- Who takes the meetings, and their calendar links
- Call recordings, if you have them. Your best talk tracks are usually already in there.

## Week one: phone setup, list, and message

Three things get built in parallel.

The phone setup. I pick a dialer that fits the list and the team, and decide between parallel and power dialing. Numbers get provisioned and registered so they don't show up as "spam likely," and I check them before the first dial. Local presence goes in where it helps.

The list. I build or clean it, verify the numbers, and score accounts so the best ones get called first. Phone coverage on the list matters more than anything else this week.

The message. A talk track built from your closed deals: why buyers started looking, what made it urgent, what almost stopped them. Then short stories by industry and persona, objection answers, a voicemail worth calling back, and a plan for gatekeepers.

By Friday you have a dial-ready list, numbers that ring clean, and a script you've read and signed off on.

## Week two: first dials

Depending on the engagement, I make the calls, your reps do, or we split them. I take phone work for one or two clients at a time so I can be on the calls myself, not just reading reports about them.

The first week of dialing is for learning, not volume. Every call gets logged: no answer, wrong number, gatekeeper, conversation, meeting. Meetings go straight onto your calendar, and the activity syncs into your CRM so nothing lives in a spreadsheet only I can see.

## Week three: find what's broken

Now we read the funnel one stage at a time, because each stage breaks for a different reason.

- Few pickups usually means a list or number problem, not a script problem.
- Plenty of conversations but few meetings points at the message or the persona.
- Meetings booked that don't happen points at how they're booked and confirmed.

I change one thing at a time so we know what worked.

## The last week: run it clean and decide

The last week runs at full pace on the fixed setup. Then you get a short report: dials, pickups, conversations, meetings booked, meetings held, cost per meeting, what we changed and why, and what I'd do next.

There are three honest outcomes:

1. Scale it. The math works. We plan the hires or keep the program running.
2. Adjust it. Something showed promise, like one segment or one title. We test that next.
3. Stop. Calling isn't the channel for this list. That's a fine result if it took a few weeks instead of a year and a hire.

## What you keep

The playbook, written so it runs without me. The list with every outcome on it. The dialer and number setup. The activity history in your CRM. If you go on to hire SDRs, I can help with screens and ramp plans, and they start with something that already works.

## Is this a fit?

It tends to fit when your deal size can pay for phone time, your buyers have numbers you can actually reach, and you want an answer in weeks. It's a poor fit if your deal size can't cover the cost of calling, or if direct numbers for your buyers don't exist.
